Work done in this week
Sections 4 and 5 of the review paper were drafted this week, together with the dataset comparison table and the two figures they refer to. Section 4 (datasets and benchmarks) runs to 996 words and cites 26 references: it opens with a subsection recording how the review itself was conducted — six databases, eight query seeds, and the record count at each stage (101 records identified, 26 duplicates removed, 75 screened by title and abstract, 74 included and 1 excluded) — then traces how evaluation data has moved from the GAN-era ForenSynths protocol of training on ProGAN and testing on eleven unseen generators, through the diffusion-era GenImage and ArtiFact collections, to in-the-wild sets such as Chameleon and TWIGMA. Its third subsection records the known problems with this data as the source papers state them: the JPEG-compression and image-size bias in GenImage, whose removal raises cross-generator accuracy by more than eleven points; the shortcut a single-generator dataset such as CIFAKE leaves open; the staleness that follows from fixing a generator list at publication; and the twenty-seven-point drop between benchmark accuracy and accuracy on Chameleon's realistic images. The dataset comparison table sets out 11 datasets across seven columns — dataset, introducing study, year, scale, real-image source, generators covered, and the limitation its authors state — with every cell copied from the extraction records; 15 cells are left blank because the records state no value, rather than being filled with a plausible guess. Section 5 (evaluation metrics and reporting practice) runs to 688 words and cites 11 references, defining accuracy, precision, recall, F1, false positive rate, AUC, average precision and mean average precision as eight numbered equations, and then examining how the surveyed studies actually report — different metric combinations over different test suites, uncalibrated decision thresholds, and averages that can hide a total failure on a single generator. Two figures were produced as SVG, the search-flow diagram for Section 4 and a metrics overview panel showing the confusion matrix with the headline formulas for Section 5; both were checked as well-formed files with selectable text so that they survive export into the report. Sections 1 to 5 are now drafted at roughly 6,970 words, supported by one comparison table and two figures. The master literature comparison table covering all 48 primary studies, and Section 6 (comparative analysis) built from it, are scheduled next.
