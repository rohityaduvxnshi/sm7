Work done in this week
The master literature comparison table was built this week, and Section 6 (comparative analysis) was drafted from it. The table gives all 48 primary studies one row each, ordered by year, across the eight columns fixed in the project brief: study, detection approach, model or architecture, dataset used, generators covered, reported accuracy or AUC, whether a cross-generator test is reported, and the limitation the authors themselves state. Every cell was compressed from the extraction table rather than re-read from the papers, and the finished table was then checked against that extraction table by script — all 48 rows matched, with no mismatch in author strings, accuracy figures or cross-generator flags. Section 6 runs to 1,532 words and cites 44 references, and is organised around counts tallied directly from the table so that each claim stays re-checkable: 36 of the 48 studies report a cross-generator evaluation; 20 cover GAN-era generators only, 8 diffusion only and 19 both; 15 train on the same ProGAN distribution; and roughly 38 report accuracy, 14 average precision and 9 AUC. The synthesis traces the chronological arc of the field — near-perfect results on a single artefact, transfer-learning CNNs, the accuracy collapse that followed the arrival of diffusion images, methods that classify a derived input, and detectors built on frozen foundation-model features — and then asks what the rows with strong cross-generator numbers have in common. It also examines what the headline figures hide, and locates explainability in only four of the 48 rows. The section closes by identifying the one combination that no surveyed study reports: several ImageNet-pretrained CNN backbones compared against a ViT benchmark under a single protocol, with explanation analysis and a cross-generator test — the gap Section 7 will formalise. Sections 1 to 6 are now drafted at roughly 8,500 words, supported by the master comparison table, the dataset comparison table and two figures. Section 7 (challenges and gap analysis), Section 8 (conclusion) and the abstract are scheduled next.
