Work done in this week
Section 2 (Background) of the review paper was drafted in full this week, at 1,024 words citing 16 verified references. It explains how a generative adversarial network trains a generator and a discriminator in opposition, how diffusion models generate an image through a fixed forward noising process and a learned reverse denoising process, and how latent diffusion moves that process into the compressed latent space of an autoencoder and admits text conditioning through cross-attention. The second half traces where detectable artefacts come from: the traces repeated upsampling leaves in the frequency spectrum, fingerprints specific to an individual model rather than a generator family, the signature of the fixed decoder shared across a latent diffusion system's output, and the fragility of all of these under JPEG compression and ordinary post-processing. The section closes by connecting artefact origin to the cross-generator generalisation problem introduced in Section 1, since evidence that follows from one generator's upsampling operator or decoder is removed, not merely made harder to find, when a new generator changes either. The reference infrastructure was also completed: a BibTeX file covering all 74 included references, keyed by the study identifiers already used in the section drafts. Each of the 74 identifiers was then opened and checked against its source record — arXiv metadata records, Crossref DOI records and one PMLR proceedings page — with one line recorded per reference in the verification log; all 74 are now verified and none pending. One publication-year error was corrected in the process: the LASTED paper had been recorded as 2025, but its DOI resolves to a journal version dated June 2026, and the reference files were updated accordingly.
