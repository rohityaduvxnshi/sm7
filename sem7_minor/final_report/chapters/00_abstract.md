This report presents the Semester 7 minor project, a systematic review of deep learning methods for detecting AI-generated still images. Cheap, publicly available text-to-image generators now produce pictures that are ever harder to tell from photographs by eye. The resulting misinformation, fabricated evidence, impersonation and contamination of web-scraped image collections outpace manual inspection, making automated detection a practical necessity. Under a search protocol fixed before collection, eight query seeds were run through the search sources the protocol names. The 48 primary studies, published from 2019 to 2025, were each read in full, with their details recorded in a fixed-field extraction table; video and audio deepfakes were excluded. Grouped by where a detector's evidence comes from, the surveyed approaches form five families: handcrafted and frequency-domain forensics, convolutional neural networks adapted by transfer learning, transformer and other attention-based models, detectors that reuse frozen vision-language features, and explainability-focused work. Benchmarks, datasets and evaluation metrics receive the same comparative treatment. The main finding is that cross-generator generalisation is a structural problem, since a large part of what a detector learns reflects a single generator's design. Reported accuracy drops from 99.9% on a detector's training generator to 54.9% on one unseen generator, and from 92.77% to 65.77% when realistic in-the-wild images replace a standard benchmark. Three properties recur in the strongest cross-generator results: frozen general-purpose representations, inputs derived from the image, and diverse training data. Of the 48 studies, 36 report a cross-generator evaluation and four an explanation analysis, yet almost none combine the two. This gap motivates the experimental study proposed for Semester 8.

Keywords: AI-generated image detection; transfer learning; convolutional neural networks; vision transformer; explainable AI; synthetic image forensics
