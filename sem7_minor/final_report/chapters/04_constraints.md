# 4. CONSTRAINTS

## 4.1 Project Constraints

Nine constraints shaped how the project was planned and carried out; they arise from the NTCC course guidelines, the review protocol, the Semester 7 scope rule that makes the deliverable a literature review, and the state of the published literature.

1. **Fixed twelve-week window.** The NTCC Minor Project (ETMN100) ran in a fixed window of 82 days, from 20 July to 9 October 2026, divided into twelve weeks running Monday to Sunday, with a short final week from 5 to 9 October. For every week a one-page Weekly Progress Report (WPR) naming all three members was due on Amizone by the following Wednesday, and the eligibility rule asks for 90% of the WPRs to be submitted and 80% of them to be satisfactory. The work therefore had to be broken into weekly steps that each ended in something reportable; Section 6.1 gives the resulting timeline.
2. **No experiments in the minor project.** The Semester 7 scope rule limits the minor project to a literature review, so it runs no experiments and reports no results of the team's own. Every accuracy, AUC or other score quoted in this report is the figure its original authors published, obtained with their own data, settings and test protocols; the review can check that a figure has been copied faithfully from the paper, but it cannot reproduce it. Running the experiments that the review argues for is the task of the Semester 8 major project (Section 14.3).
3. **Inconsistent reporting.** The surveyed studies report different metrics, among them accuracy, average precision, mean average precision and AUC, computed over different sets of test generators, so two headline figures from different papers seldom measure the same thing. Since one metric cannot be rebuilt from another once a paper is published, and threshold-based figures such as accuracy usually rest on a default operating point, many results can be placed side by side but not ranked against each other (Section 11.2).
4. **Access to full texts.** The protocol excluded any study whose full text could not be accessed, and every primary study was analysed from its full text rather than its abstract. Relevant work that the team could not open in full therefore had to stay outside the review. The protocol also limited the review to papers written in English.
5. **A fast-moving field.** Because the field moves quickly, the generator lists and benchmarks used in detection studies go out of date soon after they appear (Section 13.3). The database searches were run in the literature collection round of Week 2 (27 July to 2 August 2026), and work that appeared after that round is not covered. Preprints were admitted only if they were well cited, and one preprint with a single citation was excluded for failing this test.
6. **Citation integrity.** No reference could be cited until its identifier, whether a DOI, an arXiv record or a proceedings page, had been opened and its title and first author matched against the screening record. All 74 references in the project bibliography passed this check, through the arXiv metadata record, the Crossref record of the DOI or, in one case, the PMLR page of the proceedings, and the same pass found and corrected one wrong publication year (Section 8.6).
7. **Originality.** The Amity guidelines require the report to be written by the students in their own words and allow a similarity index of no more than 15% on Turnitin or a similar tool; anything over that limit has to be rewritten. The guide first screens the report with open-source software and the final version then goes through Turnitin on Amizone, so the report has to be the team's own writing, with every source it draws on cited.
8. **Report format.** Presentation follows the Amity report format: English text on A4 paper, side margins of 1.25 inch and top and bottom margins of 1.0 inch, line spacing of 1.5, and references cited in the text by surname and year and listed alphabetically. Within those margins the two widest tables, the dataset comparison in Chapter 10 and the master comparison of the 48 primary studies in Chapter 12, would not fit across a portrait page, so both are set in landscape orientation.
9. **Computing resources.** An ordinary laptop with an internet connection was enough for searching, reading, drafting and checking (Section 5.4), because a literature review trains no models and so needs no GPU. No hardware was bought for the project.
