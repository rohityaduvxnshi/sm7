# 6. PROJECT MANAGEMENT APPROACH

The project was run as twelve weekly stages that together covered the NTCC window, from 20 July to 9 October 2026. Each stage was planned around a defined piece of work. Its progress was then written up in a Weekly Progress Report (WPR) on the official one-page proforma, which names all three members, and each WPR was due on Amizone by the Wednesday following its week. The portal entries that went with the uploads also set out the target for the week, what had been achieved and the work planned next. The weekly reports also bear on eligibility, because the Amity guidelines require 90% of WPRs to be submitted and 80% to be judged satisfactory. Two progress reviews served as milestones beyond the weekly reports. The first was a review presentation on 27 August 2026, for which the NTCC project diary and Project Progress Report 1 were prepared. The second rested on Project Progress Report 2, which is dated 1 October 2026 and reports progress up to the end of Week 10. The work was carried out under the supervision of the faculty guide, Dr. Richa Gupta.

## 6.3 Project Risk Assessment

A risk register lists the events that could stop a project from reaching its goals, grades each by how serious and how likely it is, and records the step taken to contain it; Table 6.3 gives the register for this project.

Table: 6.3 | Project risk assessment
| Risk | Risk level (L/M/H) | Likelihood (L/M/H) | Mitigation strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project size | M | M | Scope fixed to still images and to studies from 2019–2026; detailed comparison limited to 48 primary studies |
| Incomplete search coverage | H | M | Search sources and eight query seeds fixed in the protocol; candidates confirmed on publisher and proceedings pages; counts kept at each stage (101 records found, 75 left once 26 duplicates were removed) |
| Wrong or unverifiable citations | H | M | Only keys from the screening list cited; one BibTeX file keyed by screening ID; every one of the 74 identifiers opened and matched to its record (74 verified, none pending); one year error corrected |
| Transcription errors in reported figures | H | M | Figures copied only from the extraction table; a script compared the master table with it, and all 48 rows agreed; 15 dataset-table cells left blank where no value was recorded |
| Misreading what a study tested (for example, a cross-dataset test taken as cross-generator) | H | M | Ten doubtful extraction cells settled from the full texts; two cross-generator flags changed to No and those studies described accordingly |
| Inaccessible full texts | M | L | arXiv searched alongside publisher and proceedings pages, on which records were also confirmed; all 48 primary studies read in full |
| Inconsistent metrics across studies | M | H | Eight metrics defined with equations (Chapter 11); each study's reported figure kept as stated; mismatched metrics, thresholds and averages analysed (Section 11.2) |
| Fast-moving field | M | H | Studies up to 2026, arXiv included; papers found during writing screened before being cited; literature base unchanged from Week 9; staleness treated as a challenge (Section 13.3) |
| Timeline slippage | H | M | Twelve weekly stages with set outputs and per-section word budgets; a WPR by each following Wednesday; each drafting stage reviewed before its WPR; two progress reviews |
| High similarity index | H | M | Drafts treated as working versions pending a rewrite in the team's own words; an earlier draft sent for the departmental plagiarism check; final report to be checked on Turnitin against the 15% limit |
| Late availability of the report format | M | H | Draft deliberately kept in a clean generic style, ready for restyling; DOCX source retained; this report prepared in the Amity format after the window |
| Scope creep into experiments | H | L | Minor project limited to the review, with no experiments and no results of the team's own; planned experiments stated only as Semester 8 future work; drafts checked for own-results claims |

Several of these mitigations were routine checks built into the work rather than one-off actions: each drafting stage from Week 4 to Week 11 was reviewed before its WPR was prepared, with citation keys compared against the screening list, numbers against the extraction table, and the text searched for any claim of results of the team's own. Some of the listed risks did occur. One publication year in the reference records was wrong and was corrected; full-text checking changed the cross-generator flag of two studies to No, and the comparison uses these values; and the official report format was still unavailable when the window closed, so the final formatting was done in this report.
